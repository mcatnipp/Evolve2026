# Lead-generation funnels (Russell Brunson model), built September 8, 2026

Directed by Marc Atnipp (FDI Creative): every funnel on the site is to follow the Brunson lead-generation
model. This document records what was built, how each funnel flows, the follow-up sequences the CRM
should run, and what to measure. Everything on the pages is fact-bound to the approved claims.

## 1. The value ladder

| Rung | What the visitor gets | Where | Cost to them |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Bait | Eight technical Insights, readable in full | `/insights/` | Nothing |
| 2. Lead magnet | Five PDF guides and checklists (the same governed content, formatted to share) | `/resources/` squeeze pages | Name, work email, company, role |
| 3. Application | Project brief with qualifying questions; service request; support request | `/start-a-project/`, `/request-service/`, `/support/` | A five-minute brief |
| 4. Conversation | Planning discussion with an Evolve engineer, routed by lifecycle stage | Off-site | Their time |
| 5. Engagement | Design & Build, Power Generation, Service & Maintenance | Contract | The project |
| 6. Continuity | Monitoring, maintenance and long-term support after turnover | `/maintenance/` | Agreement |

The site keeps every rung one click from the next. The proof (2,200+ projects, 5.3+ GW, 20+ years,
zero injuries recorded since 2010) appears at the rung where a visitor decides to climb.

## 2. Funnel map

### Funnel A: Guide funnel (hook, story, offer)

1. **Hook**: a direct-response H1 and a mid-page "free guide" band on every service page (`FUNNELS` in
   `data.py`, rendered by `funnel_markup()` in `build.py`), placed after the first section, before the
   visitor has scrolled past the argument.
2. **Squeeze page** `/resources/<guide>/`: hook headline, three-sentence promise, the form directly under
   the hero (four fields), then "What is inside", "Who it is for" and two Direct answers. The hero button
   scrolls to the form. No exit links in the form band beyond the global navigation.
3. **Thank-you page** `/resources/<guide>/thank-you/` (`noindex`): download button in the hero, "Read it
   online", then the Brunson thank-you offer: three "what to do next" steps and the application CTA that
   matches the guide (planning discussion, modular project, AI data center, commissioning scope, or
   maintenance discussion). One-line follow-up promise, opt-out in one reply.
4. **Application** `/start-a-project/?interest=…` pre-selects the interest; the brief now qualifies on
   facility type, target capacity, stage, site status and timeline.
5. **Follow-up**: the sequences in section 4, run from the CRM once forms are routed (ISSUE-102).

### Funnel B: Application funnel (project brief)

Hero hook → "Useful information to include" → brief with qualifying selects → `/thank-you/` with the
"What happens next" ladder (routing, engineer review, emergency line) and two guide cards for the wait.
The ladder deliberately avoids response-time promises (ledger CLM-031).

### Funnel C: Service funnel (operating facilities)

Header emergency line (24/7 emergency service, 832-375-0099, support email, support portal) →
`/request-service/` form → `/thank-you/` ladder. Maintenance pages offer the maintenance guide.

### Funnel D: Insight funnel

Article → "Get this guide as a PDF" band (before the closing) → squeeze page → thank-you → application.
The Insights hub also lists the five guides after the article cards.

## 4. Follow-up sequences (for the CRM)

Brunson's model sends value first, then the offer. Every email below is plain text, from a named Evolve
engineer, and makes no promise the approved claims cannot support. Send only after routing and consent
handling are approved (ISSUE-102).

### Guide sequence (all five guides; swap the guide name and the matching application CTA)

| Day | Subject | Body in one line | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Your [guide] from Evolve | Link to the PDF again; one sentence on how to use it; who is writing. | Download |
| 2 | The one item on the [guide] most teams skip | Name the decision that is usually deferred (utility position, interface ownership, acceptance criteria, program scope) and why it costs the most late. | Read the Insight |
| 5 | How Evolve runs that decision | Two paragraphs on the lifecycle: the same team plans, designs, builds, powers and maintains, so the decision is made once. Proof line. | Application CTA |
| 9 | A question for you | One question: which item on the checklist can you not answer yet? Reply in a sentence; an engineer answers. | Reply |
| 14 | Last note from me | Offer the planning discussion plainly; say the sequence ends here; opt-out line. | Application CTA |

### Application sequence (project brief submitted)

| Day | Subject | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | We received your brief | Confirm receipt, name the routing team by lifecycle stage, restate that a person follows up, give the emergency line for operating facilities. |
| 1 | What to have ready | The information that makes the first conversation useful (facility type, capacity target, site status, power position, milestone). |
| After the call | Recap and next decision | Written recap, the next decision, who owns it. |

### Service sequence (service request submitted)

| Day | Subject | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Your service request | Confirm receipt; repeat the emergency line; ask for the equipment list if not provided. |
| 7 | Building the program around your equipment | Offer the maintenance guide; one sentence on documented visits producing decisions. |

## 5. What to measure

- Squeeze-page conversion (visits to form submissions) per guide; target the site-selection checklist first.
- Thank-you-page click-through to the application CTA.
- Project-brief completion rate and the distribution of capacity, stage and timeline answers.
- Guide-to-brief conversion within 30 days (needs CRM matching on email).
- Split test of the H1 variants recorded in `docs/headline-test-2026-09-08.md` once analytics and consent are approved (ISSUE-107).
