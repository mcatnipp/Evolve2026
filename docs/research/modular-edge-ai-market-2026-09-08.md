## 2. What buyers search for and ask

**Keyword phrases being targeted (inference from what OEMs, integrators and content publishers such as ModulEdge, PodTech, Inflect and Introl publish in 2026; no volume data):**
"modular data center cost per MW," "how long does it take to deploy a modular data center," "modular vs traditional data center," "prefabricated modular data center AI," "AI-ready modular data center," "liquid cooling ready data center," "containerized data center 1 MW," "edge data center site selection," "micro data center," "modular data center lead time," "UL 2755 modular data center," "NEC 646," "liquid cooling retrofit existing data center," "800 VDC data center," "data center waste heat reuse," "modular data center as a service."

**RFP and contract criteria (fact, from published guidance):**
- Measurable specifications and acceptance criteria (PUE, cooling capacity, reliability), acceptance-test timeframes and remedies; warranties covering structure, hardware, embedded software and connectivity; lifecycle support with spare-parts availability and software-update policy; SLAs with response times by severity; exit and default terms (Vinson & Elkins via Data Centre Review, May 16, 2025).
- Evaluation red flags: undefined interfaces, generic code-compliance claims, component-only FAT, no transport study, poor maintenance access, uncontrolled software versions, handover that starts only after commissioning (UL/xometry-cited summaries, 2025-2026; Salas O'Brien, Aug. 27, 2026 makes the same points: "factory-tested does not mean system-coordinated" and modules can "arrive compromised" after shipping).
- Edge buyers ask: maximum power per rack, cooling design at density, carrier options and fiber diversity, deployment timeline, security certifications (Netrality, Dec. 10, 2025); public-sector RFPs for colocation and edge capacity exist (e.g., Pennsylvania Treasury RFP25-002, service start by Mar. 1, 2026).

**Technical questions a qualified buyer brings to a modular/AI conversation (compiled from the sources above):**
1. Which systems are factory-built (power skids, electrical rooms, cooling modules, IT pods, data halls) and which stay site-built?
2. Is the module UL 2755 listed / NEC 646 compliant, and how does that change the AHJ process?
3. What FAT is performed, at what load and duration, can it be witnessed remotely, and what is re-verified after transport?
4. What rack-density range, cooling architecture (air, rear-door, direct-to-chip, immersion) and ASHRAE W-class does the design support; where are the CDUs; what facility water is required?
5. Is it 800 VDC-ready or reference-architecture aligned (NVIDIA DSX, OEM reference designs)?
6. Transport: dimensions, weight, oversize permits, crane and pad requirements, wind/seismic ratings.
7. Expandability: how are future modules added, and what interfaces are pre-provisioned?
8. Long-lead equipment strategy (switchgear, transformers, generators, CDUs).
9. Warranty, spares, remote monitoring and service after turnover.
10. Water use, heat-reuse options and reporting readiness.
11. Financing: purchase, lease, rental or as-a-service.
