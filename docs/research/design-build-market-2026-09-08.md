## 2. What buyers search for and ask

**Method.** No keyword-volume dataset was available (baseline RQ-001 still open). Phrases come from SERP composition observed Sep 8, 2026, competitor FAQ headings and RFP/contract guidance; treat them as directional (**Inference**).

- **Partner-finding:** "data center construction company," "data center general contractor," "mission critical contractor," "hyperscale data center contractor," "data center design build Texas / Dallas / Houston." Results are ENR-ranked nationals and listicles (Buildermuse, ConstructionPlacements).
- **Cost and time:** "how much does it cost to build a data center," "cost per MW 2026," "how long does it take to build a data center," "data center construction timeline." Answered today by content sites and one DFW design-builder (Terrapin) with a cost guide and instant estimator; no lifecycle firm owns them.
- **Delivery method:** "OFCI vs CFCI," "data center commissioning levels," "integrated systems testing," "prefabricated electrical rooms," "powered shell," "speed to power," "behind-the-meter power," "ERCOT large load interconnection," "transformer lead time."
- **Retrofit:** "live data center retrofit," "data center power upgrade," "crypto mine conversion to AI data center" (Clayco publishes a Texas conversion).

**RFP and prequalification criteria (Fact).**
- Reported hyperscale thresholds: EMR **0.85 or below** (some 0.75), TRIR **below 2.0, preferably below 1.0**, general liability **$5M+** per occurrence with **$10M+** umbrellas, bonding **$10M+**, three years of audited financials, current ratio above 1.3, 3–5 data-center projects in five years. (Buildermuse, Apr 10, 2026; content site, medium confidence). Hyperscale owners want documented programs, leading-indicator tracking and supervisor-training evidence before award. (ABC Carolinas, 2026)
- Owner interview questions: MW delivered in three years; PUE achieved and validated; which MEP trades are self-performed; a recent long-lead mitigation example; how construction networks are secured during commissioning; "Can you share a sample integrated systems test report?" (Cadence, undated)
- Contract guarantees: Tier III/IV compliance, contracted MW and temperature/humidity under full load, N+1 or 2N, IST under simulated failure before occupancy, dated completion with liquidated damages; owners are advised to lock long-lead orders at signing, tie warranty start to Tier certification and final payment to IST. (Womble Bond Dickinson, Dec 5, 2025)
- OFCI: tie each owner-furnished item to schedule predecessors/successors, define care-and-custody transfer, spell out assembly and final connections, validate submittals against delivered equipment, assign storage and staging, delay technology-specific purchases, allocate delay risk. (Cymcor, Jun 27, 2025)
- Turnover: as-builts, one-lines, commissioning and factory records, warranty start/end dates and escalation paths, asset-tag directory, MOP/SOP/EOP training, commissioning baselines passed to maintenance. (Cadence, undated)
- Selection is qualifications-driven with price one factor among many (review of 66 CMAR and design-build RFQ/RFPs, ASCE J. Struct. Des. Constr. Pract. 30(2)).

## 4. Blind spots on the review site

Checked by reading the pages and grepping the copy packages ("0 mentions" = absent from Design & Build, Solutions and Insights copy).

1. **Speed to power and interconnection.** Pages say power "should be addressed during planning" but never use the buyer's term or touch utility interconnection, SB 6, energization sequencing or bridge power (gated, RQ-003). Only the site-selection Insight mentions interconnection.
2. **Long-lead equipment and OFCI/OFE.** 0 mentions of "long-lead," "OFCI" or "owner-furnished." Procurement is abstract: no lead-time reality, responsibility matrix, factory-witness or vendor-verification language. The most concrete 2026 anxiety and the easiest to address with market facts.
3. **Commissioning specificity.** The service page declines to define levels; buyers ask for sample IST reports and contract around IST. Gated (RQ-004), but the market-answerable Levels 1–5/IST explainer belongs on the service page, not only in the Insight.
4. **Safety proof format.** "Zero injuries since 2010" is approved, but buyers screen EMR/TRIR and competitors publish EMR (Clayco 0.46, IES 0.56). 0 mentions of EMR, TRIR, OSHA or a named program; an undefined "zero injuries" line may draw skepticism in prequalification.
5. **Workforce and self-perform scope.** No headcount, self-performed trades or shop capacity; competitors quantify (150+, 500+, 1,100+).
6. **The Houston shop as proof.** No page, photos or numbers (square footage, modules or MW fabricated, factory testing, listings), which is the modular specialists' primary proof.
7. **Digital delivery.** 0 mentions of BIM, VDC, LOD, clash detection or COBie handover.
8. **Warranty and post-turnover.** 0 mentions of "warranty"; Maintain is the only post-turnover content, while owners tie warranty start and final payment to commissioning milestones.
9. **Cost transparency.** No cost-driver education; the cost and timeline queries are captured by others.
10. **Permitting, water, community.** Only in the Responsible Development Insight; Greenfield defers site, permitting and utility scope without linking a checklist; no Texas policy or water-strategy content.
11. **Sustainability.** 0 mentions of LEED, embodied carbon, EPDs or waste diversion; Meta reports 100% of owned data centers LEED Gold or higher and 91% construction-waste diversion (Meta Sustainability, 2025 report); Clayco publishes a 75% target.
12. **Certifications and prequalification readiness.** No ISO, Uptime, licensing, bonding, insurance or prequalification-platform statement, and no "request our prequalification package" path.
13. **Delivery-model clarity.** "The exact contractual model can vary" is the only statement; buyers compare design-build, progressive design-build, CMAR, EPC and GMP.
14. **Financing and leasing.** Not addressed; leased modular units are appearing in Texas (Energy Vault/Crusoe, Snyder, 10-Q for Q2 2026). Low priority; ask.
15. **Case-study substitutes.** Competitors use "Confidential Data Center (City, ST) — MW/sf" entries, anonymous testimonials and named leaders; the site has three figures and a leadership list without biographies.
16. **Texas and Houston presence.** 0 mentions beyond the address; competitors publish Texas offices, projects and a DFW cost page.
17. **Intake path.** No attachment or NDA route on the project form; "the appropriate confidential business process" is undefined. HITT accepts files; Brasfield & Gorrie and Clayco route to a named leader.

## 5. Recommended actions

**A. Site changes we can make now with approved facts (market-sourced; no new Evolve claims)**
1. Add a "What the 2026 equipment market means for your schedule" block to Preconstruction & Procurement: dated lead-time and price facts (Wood Mackenzie, T&T; JE Dunn as competitor-reported) plus a neutral OFCI/CFCI responsibility explainer (Cymcor points), framed as questions to settle with Evolve.
2. Add "speed to power" vocabulary and a Texas large-load interconnection explainer (SB 6, proposed PUCT fees and security, Governor's audit) to Planning & Feasibility and Greenfield, linking to the Power hub and the site-selection Insight.
3. Publish a sourced "How to evaluate a data-center design-build partner" Insight (Cadence, Womble, Buildermuse criteria) that includes "what to ask Evolve."
4. Put a Levels 1–5/IST glossary block on Commissioning & Turnover as market education.
5. Add "What the turnover package should contain," including warranty-tracking items, to Commissioning & Turnover and Brownfield, linking to Maintain.
6. Add a "What drives data-center cost in 2026" explainer (Cushman, ConstructConnect, T&T) with an explicit no-pricing statement.
7. State "Houston, Texas" and "nationwide" in Design & Build body copy where the approved address already appears, so local and state intent is not orphaned.

**B. Needs a fact or approval from Evolve**
1. Three-year EMR and TRIR; the definition behind "zero injuries since 2010" (recordable? lost-time?); safety program name; any VPP or owner safety awards.
2. Workforce size, self-performed trades, Texas licenses, bonding capacity, insurance limits, prequalification platforms (ISNetworld, Avetta), any rankings.
3. Shop facts: square footage, modules or MW fabricated, factory acceptance testing, listings, transport radius, photography.
4. Commissioning scope and credentials (levels performed, IST role, third-party Cx relationships) to close RQ-004.
5. BIM/VDC platform, fabrication LOD, COBie or asset-tag handover practice.
6. Standard warranty terms and post-turnover support model.
7. Delivery models offered (design-build, progressive design-build, CMAR, EPC) and GMP practice.
8. Sustainability evidence (waste diversion, low-carbon concrete, LEED experience, water strategy).
9. Permission for anonymized project entries (city, state, MW), attributed-anonymous testimonials and leadership biographies.
10. Texas project footprint and ERCOT large-load experience statement.
11. Whether an NDA-first intake path and a prequalification-package request can be added to the form.

**C. New offer and lead-magnet ideas**
1. Long-lead equipment planner: dated lead-time ranges and decision dates by system, refreshed quarterly.
2. Texas large-load interconnection readiness checklist (SB 6/PUCT items).
3. Design-build partner evaluation scorecard with a column for Evolve's confirmed answers.
4. OFCI responsibility matrix template.
5. Turnover package and warranty-tracking checklist.
6. Modular fit assessment: a structured intake that yields a written response, not an instant estimate.
7. Shop tour or factory witness test invitation for qualified buyers (requires B.3).
8. Quarterly "delivery constraints brief" pairing market facts with questions for owners.
